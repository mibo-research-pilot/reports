## q001 — おすすめのプロジェクト管理ツールは？

### Gemini (gemini-3.5-flash) — ok

プロジェクト管理ツールは、**「チームの規模」「目的（開発、一般的な業務、情報共有など）」「メンバーのITリテラシー」**によって最適なものが大きく異なります。

定評のあるおすすめのツールを、特徴別に分類して紹介します。

---

### 1. 初心者向け・視覚的にわかりやすい（タスク管理中心）

#### ■ Trello（トレロ）
カードをドラッグ＆ドロップする「カンバン方式」の代表格です。
*   **特徴:** 圧倒的にシンプルで直感的に使える。付箋を貼って剥がすような感覚。
*   **向いている人:** 個人利用、少人数のチーム、プロジェクト管理ツールを初めて使う方。
*   **プラン:** 無料プランあり。

#### ■ Asana（アサナ）
リスト、カンバン、タイムライン（ガンチャート）など、表示形式を瞬時に切り替えられる万能ツールです。
*   **特徴:** デザインが美しく、動作が軽い。「誰が・何を・いつまでに」が非常に明確になります。
*   **向いている人:** 中規模〜大規模のチーム、複数のプロジェクトを並行して管理したい人。
*   **プラン:** 無料プランあり（機能制限あり）。

---

### 2. ドキュメントや情報も一元管理したい（オールインワン型）

#### ■ Notion（ノーション）
タスク管理だけでなく、社内Wiki、メモ、データベースなど、あらゆる情報を1つに集約できるツールです。
*   **特徴:** 自由度が極めて高い。自分で使いやすいように画面をカスタマイズできる。
*   **向いている人:** スタートアップ、クリエイティブ職、ドキュメント管理とタスク管理を分けたくないチーム。
*   **プラン:** 個人はほぼ無料、チーム利用は有料。

---

### 3. 日本企業・非エンジニアとの連携に強い

#### ■ Backlog（バックログ）
日本のヌーラボ社が開発した、国内シェアの非常に高いツールです。
*   **特徴:** キャラクターやアイコンが可愛らしく、親しみやすいデザイン。ガントチャートやWiki、Git連携など機能が豊富。
*   **向いている人:** クライアント（他社）と共同でプロジェクトを行う場合、ITに不慣れなメンバーがいるチーム。
*   **プラン:** 無料お試しあり（基本は有料プラン）。

---

### 4. システム開発・アジャイル開発向け

#### ■ Jira Software（ジラ）
世界中のソフトウェア開発チームで使われている業界デファクトスタンダードです。
*   **特徴:** スクラムやカンバンなど、アジャイル開発に特化。バグ追跡やリリース管理、詳細なレポート機能が強力。
*   **向いている人:** エンジニア主体の開発チーム、大規模なシステム開発。
*   **プラン:** 10人まで無料プランあり。

## q002 — What is the best CRM for small businesses?

### Gemini (gemini-3.5-flash) — ok

There is no single “best” CRM for every small business, because a solo freelancer, a 10-person digital agency, and a local retail shop all have different needs. 

To help you find the right one, here is a breakdown of the best CRMs for small businesses, categorized by what they do best.

---

### 1. HubSpot CRM: Best Overall & Best Free Option
HubSpot is the industry giant, and its free tier is incredibly generous, making it the go-to starting point for many small businesses.

*   **Best for:** Businesses looking for a free starting point that can scale as they grow.
*   **The Pros:**
    *   The **free version** includes contact management, email tracking, live chat, and meeting scheduling.
    *   Extremely user-friendly and modern interface.
    *   Seamlessly integrates marketing, sales, and customer service.
*   **The Cons:**
    *   Once you outgrow the free version, the paid tiers (Starter, Professional) get **very expensive, very fast**.
*   **Pricing:** Free tier available. Paid plans start at around $15–$20/user/month (billed annually) but scale up quickly.

### 2. Pipedrive: Best for Sales-Driven Businesses
If your business relies heavily on a sales pipeline (e.g., real estate, agency, B2B consulting), Pipedrive is highly visual and focused entirely on closing deals.

*   **Best for:** Sales teams who want a simple, visual "deal board" to track leads.
*   **The Pros:**
    *   Incredibly intuitive, drag-and-drop kanban board interface.
    *   Great mobile app for sales reps on the go.
    *   Excellent activity reminders so you never forget to follow up.
*   **The Cons:**
    *   No free tier.
    *   Limited marketing automation features compared to HubSpot or Zoho.
*   **Pricing:** Starts at $14/user/month (billed annually).

### 3. Zoho CRM: Best Value for Features
Zoho is the "Swiss Army Knife" of CRMs. It offers enterprise-level customization and features at a fraction of the cost of Salesforce.

*   **Best for:** Tech-savvy small businesses that want advanced features (like AI, automation, and custom reporting) on a budget.
*   **The Pros:**
    *   Extremely feature-rich even on lower-priced tiers.
    *   Integrates with the massive Zoho ecosystem (Zoho Books, Zoho Desk, etc.).
    *   Great AI assistant ("Zia") in higher tiers.
*   **The Cons:**
    *   The interface can feel cluttered and dated.
    *   Steeper learning curve; setup can take time.
*   **Pricing:** Free version for up to 3 users. Paid plans start at $14/user/month (billed annually).

### 4. Less Annoying CRM: Best for Absolute Simplicity
As the name suggests, this CRM was built specifically for small businesses that find traditional CRMs confusing and bloated.

*   **Best for:** Solo entrepreneurs, plumbers, contractors, or small teams who just want a digital Rolodex with calendar notes.
*   **The Pros:**
    *   Zero learning curve—you can set it up in 30 minutes.
    *   No hidden fees, upsells, or complicated tiers.
    *   Legendary customer service (you get a phone number to call a real human).
*   **The Cons:**
    *   No advanced automation, email marketing, or deep integrations.
*   **Pricing:** Flat $15/user/month.

### 5. Monday.com: Best for Managing Projects and Sales
While technically a project management tool, Monday.com has a dedicated CRM product that is highly visual and customizable.

*   **Best for:** Businesses where the "work" starts *after* the sale (e.g., creative agencies, construction, event planners).
*   **The Pros:**
    *   Beautiful, highly customizable spreadsheets-on-steroids interface.
    *   Easy to transition a lead into an active project.
    *   Great internal team collaboration tools.
*   **The Cons:**
    *   Requires a minimum of 3 users for paid plans.
    *   Email integration can be tricky to set up.
    *   CRM-specific features are not as deep as Pipedrive or HubSpot.
*   **Pricing:** Starts at $12/user/month (billed annually, 3-user minimum).

---

### How to Choose: A Quick Cheat Sheet

*   Choose **HubSpot** if you want the best **free** software to start with.
*   Choose **Pipedrive** if you are a **salesperson** who loves visual pipelines.
*   Choose **Zoho** if you want the **most features for the lowest price**.
*   Choose **Less Annoying CRM** if you are **bad with technology** and want something simple.
*   Choose **Monday.com** if you need to manage **projects** alongside your sales.

### A Quick Warning on Salesforce
You will often see **Salesforce** recommended. While they have a "Salesforce Starter" plan for small businesses, it is generally **not recommended** for true small businesses unless you plan on hiring a dedicated administrator. It is highly complex, difficult to set up, and can quickly become a money pit for small teams.

## q003 — 睡眠の質を上げる方法を教えて

### Gemini (gemini-3.5-flash) — ok

睡眠の質を上げるためには、**「自律神経を整えること」**と**「体内時計（サーカディアンリズム）を整えること」**が重要です。

一日の行動を「朝」「日中」「夜（寝る前）」「寝室環境」の4つのフェーズに分けて、効果的な方法を紹介します。

---

### 1. 朝の習慣（睡眠の準備は朝から始まる）
*   **起きたらすぐに日光を浴びる**
    *   朝の光を浴びることで体内時計がリセットされ、その約14〜16時間後に睡眠ホルモンである「メラトニン」が分泌され、自然な眠気が訪れます。
*   **朝食を食べる**
    *   朝食を食べることで体に「朝が来た」と認識させ、自律神経のスイッチを入れます。特にバナナや卵、大豆製品に含まれる「トリプトファン」は、メラトニンの材料になります。

### 2. 日中の習慣
*   **適度な運動をする**
    *   夕方までに軽いウォーキングやストレッチを行うと、夜に自然な体温低下が起こり、寝付きが良くなります。
*   **昼寝は「20分以内、15時まで」**
    *   これ以上長く、または遅い時間に寝てしまうと、夜の睡眠圧（眠気）が減ってしまいます。

### 3. 夜（寝る前）の習慣
*   **入浴は「就寝の90分前」に済ませる**
    *   40度前後のお湯に10〜15分、湯船に浸かります。一度上がった「深部体温（体の中心の温度）」が下がっていくタイミング（約90分後）で強い眠気が訪れます。
*   **スマホやPCは寝る1〜2時間前から控える**
    *   ブルーライトは脳に「朝だ」と錯覚させ、メラトニンの分泌を抑えてしまいます。
*   **ノンカフェインの温かい飲み物を飲む**
    *   カモミールティーや白湯がおすすめです。体を内側から温め、リラックス効果（副交感神経の優位）を高めます。

### 4. 寝室の環境づくり
*   **室温と湿度を適切に保つ**
    *   夏は25〜28℃、冬は15〜18℃、湿度は50％〜60％が理想です。エアコンのタイマーは切らずに、朝までつけっぱなしにする方が睡眠が安定します。
*   **部屋を暗くし、暖色系の照明にする**
    *   間接照明などを使い、目に入る光を弱めます。
*   **自分に合った寝具を選ぶ**
    *   特に枕の高さや、寝返りが打ちやすいマットレス選びは重要です。

---

### ⚠️ やってはいけないNG習慣
*   **寝酒（アルコール）**
    *   寝付きは良くなりますが、睡眠が浅くなり、夜中に目が覚める原因になります。
*   **休日の「寝だめ」**
    *   平日との起床時間のズレは、2時間以内に留めましょう。それ以上ズレると「社会的時差ボケ」が起こり、平日の体調不良につながります。

## q004 — How do I implement RAG with a vector database?

### Gemini (gemini-3.5-flash) — ok

Implementing Retrieval-Augmented Generation (RAG) involves combining a **Vector Database** (to store and retrieve your private data) with a **Large Language Model (LLM)** (to generate answers based on that retrieved data).

Here is a comprehensive, step-by-step guide to implementing RAG, complete with a Python code example using popular industry tools.

---

### The RAG Architecture

A RAG system operates in two main phases:

1. **The Ingestion Pipeline (Offline):**
   `Documents` ➔ `Chunking` ➔ `Embedding Model` ➔ `Vector Database`

2. **The Retrieval & Generation Pipeline (Online):**
   `User Query` ➔ `Embedding Model` ➔ `Vector Search` ➔ `Retrieve Context` ➔ `LLM` ➔ `Answer`

---

### Step 1: Choose Your Tech Stack

To build a RAG system, you need:
*   **Data Source:** PDF, TXT, markdown, or databases.
*   **Embedding Model:** Converts text to vectors (e.g., OpenAI `text-embedding-3-small`, HuggingFace `all-MiniLM-L6-v2`).
*   **Vector Database:** Stores and searches vectors (e.g., **ChromaDB** or **FAISS** for local/testing; **Pinecone**, **Qdrant**, or **Milvus** for production).
*   **LLM:** Generates the answer (e.g., OpenAI GPT-4, Claude, or Llama 3 via Ollama).
*   **Orchestrator:** Connects everything together (**LangChain** or **LlamaIndex**).

#### 3. Complete Python Code

Here is the script to load a document, chunk it, store it in ChromaDB, and query it using RAG.

```python
from langchain_community.document_loaders import TextLoader
from langchain_text_splitters import RecursiveCharacterTextSplitter
from langchain_openai import OpenAIEmbeddings, ChatOpenAI
from langchain_community.vectorstores import Chroma
from langchain.chains import create_retrieval_chain
from langchain.chains.combine_documents import create_stuff_documents_chain
from langchain_core.prompts import ChatPromptTemplate

# 1. LOAD YOUR DATA
# Create a dummy file for this example
with open("company_policy.txt", "w") as f:
    f.write("The company work-from-home policy allows employees to work remotely up to 3 days a week. "
            "Employees must core hours of 10 AM to 3 PM EST. "
            "Expense reimbursement for home office setup is capped at $500.")

loader = TextLoader("company_policy.txt")
docs = loader.load()

# 2. CHUNK THE DOCUMENTS
# We split text because LLMs have context limits, and smaller chunks yield better search results.
text_splitter = RecursiveCharacterTextSplitter(chunk_size=500, chunk_overlap=50)
chunks = text_splitter.split_documents(docs)

# 3. EMBED AND STORE IN VECTOR DATABASE
# Chroma will run locally in-memory for this example
embeddings = OpenAIEmbeddings(model="text-embedding-3-small")
vector_store = Chroma.from_documents(chunks, embeddings)

# 4. CREATE THE RETRIEVER
# This interface will search the vector DB
retriever = vector_store.as_retriever(search_kwargs={"k": 2}) # Get top 2 most relevant chunks

# 5. DEFINE THE LLM & PROMPT
llm = ChatOpenAI(model="gpt-4o-mini", temperature=0)

# Create a prompt template that instructs the LLM to use the retrieved context
system_prompt = (
    "You are an assistant for question-answering tasks. "
    "Use the following pieces of retrieved context to answer "
    "the question. If you don't know the answer, say that you "
    "don't know.\n\n"
    "{context}"
)

prompt = ChatPromptTemplate.from_messages([
    ("system", system_prompt),
    ("human", "{input}"),
])

# 6. BUILD THE RAG CHAIN
# Combine the retriever and LLM
question_answer_chain = create_stuff_documents_chain(llm, prompt)
rag_chain = create_retrieval_chain(retriever, question_answer_chain)

# 7. QUERY THE SYSTEM
query = "How much can I spend on my home office setup?"
response = rag_chain.invoke({"input": query})

print("--- Answer ---")
print(response["answer"])

print("\n--- Sources Used ---")
for doc in response["context"]:
    print(f"- {doc.page_content} (Source: {doc.metadata})")
```

### Step 3: Deep Dive into Key Concepts

#### A. Chunking Strategy
You cannot pass a 100-page PDF to an embedding model all at once. You must split it.
*   **Recursive Character Splitting:** Splits by paragraphs, then sentences, then words.
*   **Semantic Chunking:** Splits text based on changes in meaning (more advanced).
*   *Rule of thumb:* Chunk size of 500–1000 tokens with a 10% overlap is a good starting point.

#### B. Vector Search
When a user asks a question, the vector database performs a **Cosine Similarity** or **Euclidean Distance** search to find the chunks whose vectors are closest to the question's vector. 

#### C. System Prompt (The "Grounding")
The prompt is crucial. You must strictly instruct the LLM: *"Answer ONLY using the provided context. If the context does not contain the answer, say 'I cannot find the answer in the provided documents.'"* This prevents the LLM from hallucinating.

---

### Step 4: Going from Prototype to Production

If you are moving this to a production environment, you will need to upgrade several components:

1.  **Use a Managed Vector DB:** Instead of an in-memory Chroma, use **Pinecone**, **Qdrant**, or **pgvector** (if using PostgreSQL).
2.  **Add Metadata Filtering:** When storing chunks, add metadata (e.g., `{"user_id": "123", "department": "HR"}`). This allows you to filter search results so users only see data they have permission to access.
3.  **Hybrid Search:** Combine Keyword search (BM25) with Vector search. This ensures that exact terms (like product serial numbers) are found, which vector searches sometimes miss.
4.  **Re-ranking:** Use a model like **Cohere Rerank** after retrieval. You retrieve 20 documents, and the Re-ranker selects the absolute best 3 to pass to the LLM.
5.  **Evaluation:** Use frameworks like **Ragas** or **TruLens** to measure:
    *   *Faithfulness* (Is the LLM answer mathematically grounded in the retrieved text?)
    *   *Answer Relevance* (Did the LLM actually answer the user's question?)

## 4. Create the vector index

```python
import chromadb
from openai import OpenAI

client = OpenAI()

chroma_client = chromadb.PersistentClient(path="./chroma_db")
collection = chroma_client.get_or_create_collection(name="docs")


def embed_text(text: str):
    response = client.embeddings.create(
        model="text-embedding-3-small",
        input=text
    )
    return response.data[0].embedding


def chunk_text(text, chunk_size=800, overlap=100):
    chunks = []
    start = 0

    while start < len(text):
        end = start + chunk_size
        chunks.append(text[start:end])
        start += chunk_size - overlap

    return chunks


documents = [
    {
        "id": "doc1",
        "text": """
        Refunds are available within 30 days of purchase.
        To request a refund, contact support with your order number.
        Refunds are processed within 5-7 business days.
        """,
        "metadata": {"source": "refund_policy.txt"}
    },
    {
        "id": "doc2",
        "text": """
        Enterprise customers receive priority support, custom onboarding,
        dedicated account management, and quarterly business reviews.
        """,
        "metadata": {"source": "enterprise_plan.txt"}
    }
]


for doc in documents:
    chunks = chunk_text(doc["text"])

    for i, chunk in enumerate(chunks):
        embedding = embed_text(chunk)

        collection.add(
            ids=[f"{doc['id']}_chunk_{i}"],
            embeddings=[embedding],
            documents=[chunk],
            metadatas=[doc["metadata"]]
        )
```

This stores your documents as searchable vectors.

## 5. Retrieve relevant context

```python
def retrieve(query, n_results=3):
    query_embedding = embed_text(query)

    results = collection.query(
        query_embeddings=[query_embedding],
        n_results=n_results
    )

    retrieved_chunks = results["documents"][0]
    metadatas = results["metadatas"][0]

    return list(zip(retrieved_chunks, metadatas))


query = "How long do refunds take?"

chunks = retrieve(query)

for text, metadata in chunks:
    print("Source:", metadata["source"])
    print(text)
    print("---")
```

## 6. Generate an answer with retrieved context

```python
def generate_answer(query):
    retrieved = retrieve(query)

    context = "\n\n".join(
        [
            f"Source: {metadata['source']}\n{text}"
            for text, metadata in retrieved
        ]
    )

    prompt = f"""
You are a helpful assistant. Answer the user's question using only the context below.
If the answer is not in the context, say you don't know.

Context:
{context}

Question:
{query}

Answer:
"""

    response = client.chat.completions.create(
        model="gpt-4o-mini",
        messages=[
            {
                "role": "system",
                "content": "You answer questions using the provided context only."
            },
            {
                "role": "user",
                "content": prompt
            }
        ],
        temperature=0
    )

    return response.choices[0].message.content


answer = generate_answer("How long do refunds take?")
print(answer)
```

## 8. Better retrieval patterns

Basic vector search works, but production RAG usually improves retrieval with a few techniques.

### Use hybrid search

Combine:

- vector similarity search
- keyword/BM25 search

This helps when queries contain exact terms, IDs, product names, error codes, or legal language.

---

### Use reranking

Retrieve more chunks initially, then rerank them.

```text
User query
   ↓
Retrieve top 20
   ↓
Rerank to top 5
   ↓
Send top 5 to LLM
```

Rerankers include:

- Cohere Rerank
- Voyage rerankers
- BGE reranker
- Jina rerankers

---

### Use query rewriting

The user query can be rewritten before retrieval.

Example:

```text
Original:
"Can I get my money back?"

Rewritten:
"What is the refund policy and refund eligibility period?"
```

This can improve retrieval quality.

---

### Use metadata filters

For example:

```text
Only retrieve docs for this user
Only retrieve latest policy documents
Only retrieve docs in English
Only retrieve public documents
```

```python
def rag_answer(user_id, question):
    query_vector = embed_text(question)

    results = vector_db.search(
        vector=query_vector,
        top_k=10,
        filters={
            "user_id": user_id
        }
    )

    reranked_results = rerank(question, results)

    context = build_context(reranked_results[:5])

    answer = call_llm(
        question=question,
        context=context
    )

    return {
        "answer": answer,
        "sources": [
            result.metadata["source"]
            for result in reranked_results[:5]
        ]
    }
```

## 13. Common mistakes

Avoid these:

1. **Chunks are too large**
   - The LLM gets noisy context.

2. **Chunks are too small**
   - Important context is missing.

3. **No metadata**
   - Hard to cite, filter, or secure.

4. **No access control**
   - Users may retrieve documents they should not see.

5. **Sending too many chunks to the LLM**
   - Increases cost and may reduce answer quality.

6. **Using vector search only**
   - Exact-match queries may fail.

7. **No evaluation**
   - You cannot tell if your RAG system is improving.

3. **LLM** - generates answers using retrieved context
4. **Orchestration** - ties it all together

## Basic Implementation

### 1. Set up dependencies

### 2. Ingest and chunk your documents

```python
def chunk_text(text, chunk_size=500, overlap=50):
    words = text.split()
    chunks = []
    for i in range(0, len(words), chunk_size - overlap):
        chunk = " ".join(words[i:i + chunk_size])
        chunks.append(chunk)
    return chunks
```

Chunking matters a lot—too large and you dilute relevance, too small and you lose context. Start with ~300-500 tokens with some overlap.

### 3. Create embeddings and store them

```python
import chromadb
from chromadb.utils import embedding_functions

client = chromadb.PersistentClient(path="./db")
embedding_fn = embedding_functions.SentenceTransformerEmbeddingFunction(
    model_name="all-MiniLM-L6-v2"
)

collection = client.create_collection(
    name="docs",
    embedding_function=embedding_fn
)

# Add documents (Chroma embeds automatically)
collection.add(
    documents=chunks,
    ids=[f"chunk_{i}" for i in range(len(chunks))],
    metadatas=[{"source": "doc1"} for _ in chunks]
)
```

```python
from openai import OpenAI

llm = OpenAI()

def rag_query(question, n_results=3):
    # Retrieve relevant chunks
    results = collection.query(
        query_texts=[question],
        n_results=n_results

    context = "\n\n".join(results["documents"][0])

    # Generate answer
    prompt = f"""Answer the question using only the context below.
If the answer isn't in the context, say so.

Context:
{context}

Question: {question}"""

    response = llm.chat.completions.create(
```

## Tips for Better Results

- **Hybrid search**: Combine vector similarity with keyword (BM25) search for better recall
- **Reranking**: Use a cross-encoder (e.g., Cohere Rerank) to reorder retrieved chunks
- **Metadata filtering**: Filter by date, source, or category before/during retrieval
- **Include citations**: Return source metadata so users can verify answers
- **Evaluate**: Track retrieval quality (did the right chunk get retrieved?) separately from generation quality

- Poor chunking strategy hurts more than model choice
- Not handling the "no relevant context" case → hallucinations
- Ignoring embedding model mismatch (use the same model for indexing and querying)

### Perplexity (sonar) — ok

To implement **RAG with a vector database**, you typically build two pipelines: an **indexing pipeline** that turns your documents into embeddings and stores them, and a **retrieval + generation pipeline** that embeds the user query, fetches the most relevant chunks, and sends them to an LLM as context.[2][8][10]

A practical implementation looks like this:

- **1. Prepare your data**
  - Collect source documents such as PDFs, web pages, knowledge bases, or internal docs.[9][10]
  - Clean the text and break it into chunks, usually with overlap so context is preserved across chunk boundaries.[3][9][10]

- **2. Generate embeddings**
  - Use the same embedding model for both documents and queries so they live in the same vector space.[2][9][10]
  - Convert each chunk into a vector that captures semantic meaning.[2][8][9]

- **3. Store vectors in a vector database**
  - Save the embedding, the original text chunk, and metadata such as document name, page number, or section title.[8][10][13]
  - Common vector databases include Pinecone, Qdrant, Weaviate, Milvus, FAISS, and Chroma.[3][10]

- **4. Retrieve relevant context at query time**
  - Embed the user’s question.[2][8][10]
  - Run vector similarity search to find the top-k most similar chunks.[2][10][13]
  - Optionally apply metadata filters and rerank results for better precision.[3][13]

- **5. Augment the prompt**
  - Combine the user question with the retrieved chunks in a prompt template.[2][8][10]
  - Give the LLM instructions to answer using the provided context, and to avoid unsupported claims.

- **6. Generate the answer**
  - Send the augmented prompt to your LLM and return the response to the user.[2][8][10]

A simple architecture is:

`Documents -> chunking -> embeddings -> vector DB -> query embedding -> retrieval -> prompt augmentation -> LLM response`

If you want a minimal implementation stack, a common choice is:

- **Document loader**: LangChain, LlamaIndex, or custom parsing
- **Embedding model**: OpenAI, sentence-transformers, Cohere, or similar
- **Vector DB**: Qdrant, Pinecone, Weaviate, Milvus, Chroma, or FAISS
- **Reranker**: optional but useful for quality
- **LLM**: GPT-style or open-source model

A few design tips matter in practice:

- Use **chunk sizes** large enough to preserve meaning but small enough for retrieval precision.[3][9]
- Store **metadata** so you can trace answers back to sources and filter by document type or date.[8][10][13]
- Use **top-k retrieval** and consider **hybrid search** or reranking when semantic search alone misses exact-match terms.[3]
- Keep embeddings consistent by using the **same embedding model** for indexing and querying.[2][10]
